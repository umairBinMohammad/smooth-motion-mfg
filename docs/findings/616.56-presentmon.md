# Driver 616.56: what reaches the screen (PresentMon)

PresentMon 2.6.0, v1 metrics, Ghost Recon Breakpoint at 2560x1440 on the RTX
4060 Laptop, prototype 0.8.2 (15 s stock 2x, then 15 s of 3x without pacing).
Present mode was Hardware: Independent Flip throughout; nothing was dropped.

## Stock 2x (base frame ~37 ms)

| Present | In Present() | Render complete | Displayed | msFlipDelay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| generated | 0.15 ms | +23 ms | +23 ms | 0 |
| real (called 0.8 ms later) | ~22 ms | +23 ms | +41 ms | ~18.5 |

- The generated frame is shown when the graph finishes (R, ~23 ms after launch).
- The real frame's Present call blocks until R, and the **driver then delays
  its flip by about half a frame** (`msFlipDelay`). This is the software flip
  metering; the game thread does not wait for it.
- The present thread is blocked until R every frame, so CPU work and GPU work
  are serialised: base frame = CPU part (~14 ms) + GPU part (~23 ms).

## 3x without pacing (base frame ~42 ms)

Per frame, in call order (matched to the mod's log by QPC):

| Present | Called | Displayed | msFlipDelay |
|---|---|---|---|
| NvPresent generated (t = 1/3) | L+0.5 | R | 0 |
| our second generated (t = 2/3) | R+0.6 | R+13 | 12 to 14 |
| NvPresent real | R+1.8 | R+13.7 | 0 |

- The driver applied its flip delay to **the present that follows NvPresent's
  generated frame**, which is now ours, and the delay it chose was a third of
  the frame, not half: it appears to use the average present interval.
- The real frame got no delay and replaced our frame 0.7 ms later. That is the
  judder: on screen it was 2x with the generated frame at t = 1/3.

## Why waiting on the game thread fails

Any wait added on the present thread adds directly to the base frame. Holding
the real frame for a third of a frame gives T' = T + T'/3, so 1.5x the frame
time: 3 x (base / 1.5) is the same output rate as stock 2x. 0.8 held for two
thirds and ran away (40 to 136 ms), as that formula predicts.

## Consequence

The first two frames are already where they should be (R and R + T/3). Only
the real frame needs moving to R + 2T/3, and it has to be delayed on the GPU
or by the driver, never on the game thread. Prototype 0.9 queues a D3D11
fence wait on NvPresent's context in front of the real frame's present and
signals it from a pacer thread through a second device.

## Prototype 0.9 (real frame gated on the GPU)

Display order per real frame (PresentMon, matched to the log), base frame
~42 ms, so the target spacing is ~14 ms:

| Present | Shown at | Gap to previous | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| NvPresent generated (t = 1/3) | R | ~13 ms | as before |
| our generated (t = 2/3) | R + ~6.5 | ~6.5 ms | driver flip delay shrank from ~13 to ~6 ms |
| real (gated) | R + ~26 | ~20 ms | gate opened at ready + ~29 |

- The gate works: the real frame's present was held on the GPU (render
  complete moved from +1 ms to +25 ms) without any wait on the game thread
  at the gated call itself.
- But the driver's own delay for our frame adapts to the average present
  interval, which the gate changed, so our frame now arrives too early.
- The *next* NvPresent Present then blocked ~12 ms inside DXGI (msInPresentAPI),
  because the gated present filled the frame queue. That raised the base frame
  from ~37 to ~42 ms.
- 80 presents/s in 3x against 51 in 2x; display intervals alternated roughly
  13 / 6.5 / 20 ms, which is the judder.

Prototype 0.10 gates both of our frames (second generated at ready + T/3, real
at ready + 2T/3) instead of relying on the driver's delay for the first one,
and raises NvPresent's device's maximum frame latency to 4 so gated presents
do not block the game thread.

## Prototype 0.10 (both our presents gated on the GPU)

| | 2x | 3x |
|---|---|---|
| Frames shown per second | 56.0 | 59.6 |
| Display interval mean / SD | 17.9 / 3.1 ms | 16.8 / 2.2 ms |
| Base frame | ~37 ms | ~53 ms |

Spacing in 3x became even (display intervals ~13 / 16 / 18 ms, steadier than
stock 2x), but the base frame grew by almost half, cancelling the gain: a GPU
wait on NvPresent's context blocks everything queued after it, including the
cross-device fence waits the game's next frame depends on.

Prototype 0.11 drops GPU gating. The game thread runs the second warp and
returns; a pacer thread copies and presents our frame at ready + T/3 and
issues the real frame's Present (handed over by NvPresent, which gets S_OK at
once) at ready + 2T/3, with NvPresent's device in multithread-protected mode.

## Prototype 0.11 (pacer thread presents our frame and the real frame)

| | 2x | 3x |
|---|---|---|
| Frames shown per second | 53.6 | 73.9 |
| Display interval mean / SD | 18.6 / 4.2 ms | 13.5 / 11.4 ms |
| Base frame | ~35 ms | ~41 ms |

More frames, but spacing is erratic (intervals from 0.2 ms to 26 ms within a
frame). The pacer released each present on time, yet:

- the real frame's Present, issued at ready + 2T/3, blocked 12 to 20 ms inside
  DXGI and reached the screen late (ready + 33 to 44 ms against a 27 ms target);
- PresentMon still shows driver flip delays of 6 to 20 ms on some of our
  presents, and none on others;
- 30 times the game thread had to wait for the pacer before its next frame.

Every Present on this swapchain goes through NvPresent's own pacer, which adds
its own waits and flip delays based on its model of a two-image batch
(`NVP_Pacer::submitBatch(..., std::array<NVP_Image*, 2>&, ...)`). Timing
presents from outside fights that pacer instead of replacing it. The next step
is to read how `NVP_Pacer` decides waits and flip delays (static analysis of
NvPresent64.dll), then either drive it with a three-image batch or switch its
delay off for our presents.
