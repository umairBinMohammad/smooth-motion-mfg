# Driver 616.56: what reaches the screen (PresentMon)

PresentMon 2.6.0, v1 metrics, Ghost Recon Breakpoint at 2560x1440 on the RTX
4060 Laptop, prototype 0.8.2 (15 s stock 2x, then 15 s of 3x without pacing).
Present mode was Hardware: Independent Flip throughout; nothing was dropped.

## Stock 2x (base frame ~37 ms)

| Present | In Present() | Render complete | Displayed | msFlipDelay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| generated | 0.15 ms | +23 ms | +23 ms | 0 |
| real (called 0.8 ms later) | ~22 ms | +23 ms | +41 ms | ~18.5 |

- The generated frame is shown when the graph finishes (R, ~23 ms after launch).
- The real frame's Present call blocks until R, and the **driver then delays
  its flip by about half a frame** (`msFlipDelay`). This is the software flip
  metering; the game thread does not wait for it.
- The present thread is blocked until R every frame, so CPU work and GPU work
  are serialised: base frame = CPU part (~14 ms) + GPU part (~23 ms).

## 3x without pacing (base frame ~42 ms)

Per frame, in call order (matched to the mod's log by QPC):

| Present | Called | Displayed | msFlipDelay |
|---|---|---|---|
| NvPresent generated (t = 1/3) | L+0.5 | R | 0 |
| our second generated (t = 2/3) | R+0.6 | R+13 | 12 to 14 |
| NvPresent real | R+1.8 | R+13.7 | 0 |

- The driver applied its flip delay to **the present that follows NvPresent's
  generated frame**, which is now ours, and the delay it chose was a third of
  the frame, not half: it appears to use the average present interval.
- The real frame got no delay and replaced our frame 0.7 ms later. That is the
  judder: on screen it was 2x with the generated frame at t = 1/3.

## Why waiting on the game thread fails

Any wait added on the present thread adds directly to the base frame. Holding
the real frame for a third of a frame gives T' = T + T'/3, so 1.5x the frame
time: 3 x (base / 1.5) is the same output rate as stock 2x. 0.8 held for two
thirds and ran away (40 to 136 ms), as that formula predicts.

## Consequence

The first two frames are already where they should be (R and R + T/3). Only
the real frame needs moving to R + 2T/3, and it has to be delayed on the GPU
or by the driver, never on the game thread. Prototype 0.9 queues a D3D11
fence wait on NvPresent's context in front of the real frame's present and
signals it from a pacer thread through a second device.
