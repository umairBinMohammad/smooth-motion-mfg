# Driver 616.56: what the network outputs

From eight F9 captures (tracer 0.2) in Ghost Recon Breakpoint at 2560x1440,
analysed with `tools/analyze_capture.py`. Captures cover a still camera, slow
and fast pans.

## Layout

The buffer `warp_coarse_kernel` reads is 256x144 with **16 half floats per
pixel**, interleaved:

| Channels | Meaning | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0, 1 | Flow (x, y) from the generated frame into the **previous** real frame, in coarse pixels | Warping with it aligns the frames (below) |
| 2, 3 | Flow into the **current** real frame | Same; roughly the negative of 0, 1 |
| 4, 5 and 6, 7 | A second pair of flows, close to the first pair | Near-identical statistics; *role inferred*: a second sample per frame |
| 8..11 | Four logits, one per sample; the warp kernel takes their softmax as blend weights | Near zero on a still camera, up to about +-18 in motion; *inferred* from the kernel's `exp2` and four texture fetches |
| 12..15 | Zero | |

The 256x144 input buffer holds 8 half floats per pixel: previous RGB, current
RGB, two unused. The "previous" of one capture equals the "current" of the
one before it, bit for bit.

Flows are in units of the 256x144 grid; the warp's x10 factor at 1440p turns
them into screen pixels.

## Flows can be rescaled in time

Mean absolute difference between the previous frame warped by `2t * flow_prev`
and the current frame warped by `2(1-t) * flow_cur` (lower is better; t = 0.5
is what NvPresent generates):

| Capture | Unaligned | t=0.50 | t=0.33 | t=0.67 | t=0.25 | t=0.75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| set2 | 0.0231 | 0.0080 | 0.0083 | 0.0086 | 0.0085 | 0.0089 |
| set4 | 0.0474 | 0.0213 | 0.0225 | 0.0216 | 0.0241 | 0.0217 |
| set6 | 0.0581 | 0.0156 | 0.0158 | 0.0160 | 0.0158 | 0.0164 |
| set7 | 0.0344 | 0.0126 | 0.0127 | 0.0134 | 0.0130 | 0.0140 |

Rescaled flows align the two real frames almost as well at 1/3, 2/3, 1/4 and
3/4 as at the midpoint. **One network pass therefore carries enough to build
frames at other times**: sample the previous frame at `p + 2t * flow_prev`,
the current frame at `p + 2(1-t) * flow_cur`, and shift the blend weights
towards the nearer frame.

Limits of this evidence: it is measured at 256x144 on colour agreement, not on
the full-resolution result; it assumes straight-line motion between frames;
and the flow field is evaluated at the midpoint's pixel positions rather than
re-projected to time t, which will show at the edges of fast-moving objects.

## Cost

`cuGraphLaunch` plus a stream sync took 16 to 25 ms at capture time. That
includes waiting behind the game's own GPU work (base frame rate was about
25 fps in these captures), so it is an upper bound, not the network's cost.

## What is left

Generation: our own warp kernel (PTX, loaded through `cuModuleLoadData`) that
takes t. Needs the exact weight formula from `warp_coarse_kernel` and the
handles of the two full-resolution input textures and the output surface.

Presentation: NvPresent's pacer submits batches of exactly two images. Getting
extra frames on screen, evenly spaced, is now the larger unknown.
